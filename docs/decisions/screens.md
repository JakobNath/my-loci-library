# Decisions: Screens: adding books, Library, book page, Wishlist, Settings, Value

Moved word for word from `HOW_IT_WORKS.md` on 10 November 2026, in their original order (oldest first). Add new decisions on this topic at the end, in the same form: a bold title with the date, then the choice and the reason.

- **Batch add, as built (2026-11-09).** A typed ISBN, a USB scanner (which types the ISBN and Enter into the same box) and the camera all lead to the same review card. Open Library is asked first; Google Books only when Open Library knows nothing or lacks authors or publisher. Google Books now refuses all requests without a key (its daily limit for keyless use was zero on 9 November 2026), so Settings has a place for this device's free key. Work suggestions match the title without a leading article and any form of an author's name; nothing is linked unless the person chooses "Same work as". A found Open Library cover is downloaded and compressed after saving, so the next scan is ready at once (measured: 13 ms after Save, against a 200 ms target).
- **Original dates on the review card (2026-11-09).** Jakob's rule is that the first entry should be complete. The card now asks "When was this work written? (not this printing)" every time, unless the linked work already has a date, and the edition's year is labelled "Year of this printing". Saving with no date asks once; "Don't know yet" skips on purpose. Suggestions come from Open Library's record of the work behind the ISBN and its author, reasoned as follows: Open Library's earliest edition is often the first publication (modern books, and translations of modern books), but if it is later than the author's death it is only a printing, and the author's lifetime is suggested instead (Augustine's Confessions: 1482 in Open Library, so 354–430; Chemnitz's Examen: 1596, so 1522–1586); a first publication in the same year as the printing is flagged for checking. Each suggestion says where it comes from and fills nothing until tapped, which keeps the rule that only a person sets original dates; the tapped suggestion's source is kept as the dating's note. Open Library's data has errors (it gives 1928 for Bonhoeffer's Nachfolge, published 1937), which is why suggestions are labelled. The work's title is offered as the original title when it differs (Examen Concilii Tridentini, Nachfolge).
- **More on the review card (2026-11-09).** At Jakob's request: original title, translation first published (when there is a translator), series and number, set and volume, price paid, estimated value, and notes, so most books need no second visit.
- **Batch add, step by step (2026-11-10).** After phone testing Jakob found the single review card clunky, at times wordy and at times unclear, and it did not ask enough. Batch add now asks one question per screen, in the order Jakob chose: is this the book (with "Fix details" for anything missing or wrong), the same work as one already held (only when likely), when the work was written, the cover, a spine photo, the binding, where it goes, and anything else (notes, value, price paid, series, set, translation date). Steps with nothing to ask are skipped; each shows "Step n of N", a short line saying what is wanted, and large answer buttons. The online cover is now downloaded and checked while the person answers the first steps, so the cover step can say "No cover found online" (or "too small to look sharp") and offer a photo, instead of failing quietly after saving. Titles that come back all in lower case are put into title case; titles with capitals of their own are left alone.
- **Phone fit (2026-11-10).** Body text is 16 px and small text 14 px (iPhones zoom in on fields with smaller text), buttons are at least 44 px tall, and the scan camera fills the width (up to 640 px). See `docs/plan/DESIGN.md`.
- **Saved views are shared; the current view is not (2026-11-10).** A saved view (`views` in settings.json) is the same set of choices as the Library's own controls: format, sort, status, location, category, tag and "needs an original date". Saved views describe the library, so both of us see them; which view is open on a screen is this device's alone. The filter choices (every place, category and tag in use) are worked out only while the filter panel is open, so opening the Library is no slower.
- **The trash works book by book (2026-11-10).** "Move to trash" sits at the foot of the edit form, away from the book page, since it is rarely wanted. It moves the edition, its copies, and any of its works no other edition uses; a work shared with another edition stays, so that edition keeps its dates. Settings › Trash lists books and wishlist items with the day they were moved, and Restore brings back the edition, its copies and its works. There is no "empty the trash": records are never erased, so their IDs are never reused (DATA_FORMAT.md, "IDs").
- **The wishlist is edited in place (2026-11-10).** Adding or editing an item opens a small form inside the Wishlist tab (title, subtitle, authors, translator, publisher, ISBN, note) rather than a screen of its own. "I bought it" opens the normal entry form at `#/new/<item id>`, filled from the item, with its note on the new copy; saving the book moves the item to the trash, so it can be restored if the purchase falls through. Wishlist items have no cover photo yet: PLAN.md allows an optional one, and the item's `images` list is ready for it, but nothing so far has needed it. The Wishlist sorts by newest, title or author; the choice is kept on the device, starting from the shared default.
- **Managed lists (2026-11-10).** Settings › Lists shows the categories, tags, series, bindings and places in use, each with how many works, books or copies use it. Tapping one renames it on every record (including those in the trash, so a restored book matches); choosing a name already in the list merges the two. A place is renamed one part at a time ("Office" in Home › Office), and everything inside it moves with it. The binding choices offered when adding a book are listed too, even before any copy uses them, and renaming one renames the choice. The code is in `core/lists.js` and `ui/lists.js`, loaded only with Settings.
- **The Settings page (2026-11-10).** Settings has six parts: The library (shared: dates, currency, anthologies in date order, what adding asks for, default sorts, lookup sources), Lists, This device (theme, view, cover size, Logos in the main view, camera or USB scanner, beep, Google Books key), Sync, Trash, and About. Exports and the backup dates in PLAN.md's "Data and care" are added with the features themselves in phases 9 and 10, so the page never offers something that does not work yet.
- **How the shared default sort and "Logos in the main view" apply (2026-11-10).** The Library uses the shared default sort until this device picks a sort of its own. Turning "Logos books in the main Library view" on or off also switches the Library to All or Physical, so the setting does what it says at once; the Physical, Logos and All chips still change it for the moment.
- **"Anthologies, sorted by original date" (2026-11-10).** With "Under each work they hold", a view sorted by original date shows an anthology once for each different date among its works (the Loeb Plato under 399, 385 and 370 BC). Other sorts always show it once.
- **What adding asks for (2026-11-10).** "When adding, ask for these if they are missing" decides which missing fields the step-by-step add points out (author, publisher, year) and whether it asks for an original date and a binding. Title is always asked for, since a book cannot be saved without one.
- **Book page cover (2026-11-11, interface review).** On a phone the cover takes about a third of the width, so the title and facts start higher; tapping it opens the cover and spine full size in a `<dialog>`, and a tap anywhere closes it. Wide screens keep the cover column.
- **Add flow wording (2026-11-11, interface review).** Small changes only, as asked: a named step counter with the book's title, a message saying which service is being asked, one plain hint per step, and a confirmation line at the top after saving. INTERFACE_NOTES.md lists them.
- **Finding a wishlist item (2026-11-11, interface review).** Jakob asked to add wishes by search, or from an Amazon link, with a cover. The app may only talk to Open Library, Google Books and GitHub, so an Amazon link is never fetched: for printed books Amazon's address carries the ISBN-10 ("/dp/0199540144"), which `isbnInText` in `core/entry.js` reads and converts. Kindle and short a.co links carry no ISBN, and the app says so. Typing a title searches Open Library as you type (after a short pause), showing small covers to pick from; a pick fills only the title, authors and cover, because a title search finds the work, not one edition. The cover is made into the item's own picture files at once; if the edition has no cover on Open Library, the work's cover is used. When the book is bought, the new edition takes over the same picture.
- **A short Settings page (2026-11-11, Jakob).** Settings was crowded, and the trash would grow without end. The main page now holds only what anyone using the app may want: Look (theme, library view, cover size), a one-line sync status with "Sync now", and three rows leading to their own pages: Trash (with its count), Rename and merge (the managed lists), and Advanced (the shared library settings, scanning and lookups, the Google Books key, sync setup, and About). The pages are `#/settings/trash`, `#/settings/lists` and `#/settings/advanced`.
- **Book page beside the cover (2026-11-11, Jakob).** On a phone the small cover left wide empty margins. The cover now sits on the left (about a third of the width) with the title, author, dates, the book's shelf (a link to that shelf) and Edit beside it; the title is 22 px there and 30 px on wide screens.
- **The value view (2026-11-10).** Settings › Value (`#/value`) shows the total of the owned physical copies, the totals by room (the first two parts of a location, such as Home › Office), how many copies have no value yet, and the Logos total apart. "Download for an insurance agent" saves a CSV of each owned physical copy with its value, ending with the totals. A value history entry is deleted by ticking it under "Value history" in the book's edit form; several changes on one day already keep only that day's last value. The code is `core/value.js` and `ui/value.js`, loaded only when opened.
- **Sync set up as three numbered steps (2026-11-10, Jakob).** Moving his phone to the real catalog, Jakob found the single Sync form "super confusing": one form for the name and key, and the way to make a key hidden in a fold. Settings › Advanced › Sync now shows the catalog in use and the last sync first, with "Sync now", then "Connect to another catalog, or renew the key" as three boxes: Step 1, which catalog; Step 2, a button that opens GitHub's key form, with three short lines, the second naming the catalog typed in step 1 (updated as it is typed), since picking it under "Repository access" is the step people miss; Step 3, paste the key and connect. GitHub's form cannot pick the repository in advance (GitHub Docs, "Pre-filling fine-grained personal access token details using URL parameters", checked 2026-11-10), so naming it is the most the app can do. The steps reuse the entry form's boxes, so only two small spacing rules were added.
- **Library controls (2026-11-11, second tour).** Jakob found the pills plain and not quite clear. Physical, Logos and All are now one segmented switch (a rounded track with the chosen part raised on it), and below it sit Sort (with a sort icon), a Filter button (funnel icon, with a small honey count of the filters in use) and the view switch (covers, list, shelves) as a matching icon switch. On wide screens they share one row. The shelves button had been drawn without the buttons' padding, because it is a link; it now matches. New tokens `--seg-track`, `--seg-thumb` and `--shadow-thumb` give the switch its colors in light and dark. To stay within the start-up size budget, form styles used only by screens that load later moved from `app.css` to `screens.css`.
- **Covers keep their own shape (2026-11-11, second tour).** A cover picture squarer than 2:3 (Liddell and Scott) showed a band of its main color above it, because the cover box is always 2:3 and its color filled the gap. Each picture stores its shape (`ratio`), so in the grid and lists the 2:3 box is now a clear slot with the picture standing at its foot in its own shape (rows stay one height, as the long list needs); on the book page the box takes the picture's shape and the spine beside it is sized from both shapes. Pictures without a stored shape are drawn as before.
- **Library grid caption (2026-11-11, second tour).** A one-line title left a blank line before the author, because the title always took two lines' room. The room is now kept at the foot of the caption, so the author follows the title directly and rows stay one height.
- **Wishlist covers in search (2026-11-11, second tour).** Wishlist items found by search are drawn with their own covers, like books.
- **Field help and the guide (2026-11-11, second tour).** Jakob asked for explanations that are there when wanted and out of the way otherwise. A small "i" beside the label of the fields people wonder about (original title and language, category, tags, edition, translation first published, series, set, pages, languages, binding) opens one or two sentences under the field; only one is open at a time. It is a button inside the label, which does not move the cursor into the box. Settings has a short guide, "How the catalog is organized": work, edition and copy; series and sets; categories and tags, with advice on using them; original dates; and where the details come from. Both read their text from `ui/field-help.js`, so a field is explained the same way everywhere, and both load only with the screens that use them. Rejected: `<details>` inside the label (a tap on it can move the cursor into the box in some browsers) and help text always shown (too crowded).
- **Adding asks what a person knows; the laptop fills the rest (2026-11-11, Jakob).** The laptop's nightly check (`loci-enrich`) filled fields after a book was added, so whoever added it could not know what would still be empty. Jakob chose two passes. Adding now asks, one screen each and all skippable: the original language (six one-tap languages, or typed; English goes straight on, any other asks for the original title, with Open Library's work title offered), category and tags (chips of those in use, or typed), the translation (translator and "translation first published", whenever a translator was found or the language is not English), and this copy (acquired, price paid, value, markings, notes). The language, category and tags are skipped for a book linked to a work already in the library, and for a collection. Series, set and volume left the steps: series comes from the lookups and the laptop, and sets have their own way in. The new steps are drawn in `ui/add-questions.js`, so `ui/add.js` keeps the order and saving. Tags are offered as chips up to the 20 most used, with every tag suggested as it is typed, since a real library may use hundreds.
- **"Needs details" (2026-11-11).** A Library filter, a line on each book's page ("Still missing: pages, publisher · Edit") and a row on the Settings main page with the count, which opens the Library with the filter on. A book needs details when it is physical and lacks a publisher, year, pages or binding (`missingDetails` in `core/library.js`); it is worked out from the fields, so the data format is unchanged. Two small changes from the handoff: Logos books are never listed, because their details come from the Logos export and nobody fills them in at a shelf; and the list follows the chosen sort rather than a fixed "oldest first", which would have overridden the Sort control ("Date added" is one tap away).
- **Adding a set (2026-11-11).** "Add a set of volumes" on the add screen asks once for the set's title, publisher, year, edition, general editors and binding. A banner then stays on the scan screen until "Finish the set", kept while the app is open (like the shelf being filled), so a look at the Library mid-set loses nothing. Each scanned or typed volume takes the set's title, publisher, edition, binding and editors (`withSetFacts` in `core/entry.js`), but keeps the year its lookup found, because a set's volumes often appear over decades (Luther's Works, 1955 to 1986); the set's year fills in only when none is found. A "Which volume is this?" step suggests the next number ("31" then "32", "I/1" then "I/2"); the previous volume's language, category and tags are preselected. "Paste the volumes held" reads one volume per line ("7 Plato"; a line that is only a number becomes "Luther's Works, vol. 33") and puts them all on one shelf, in line order.
- **Arrange on the shelf (2026-11-11).** The shelf view has an Arrange button: tap a book (it is raised and outlined in honey), then one of the thin gaps that appear between the spines. Only the moved copy gets a new shelf order (`keysAfterMove` in `core/shelf-order.js`). When some copies on the shelf have no shelf order yet, or two share one (two devices adding at once), the whole shelf is given fresh orders once, in its new order. Done leaves the mode; spines do not open their book while arranging.
- **Collections on screen, planned (2026-11-11, Jakob).** For phase 15: "Is this the book?" offers "Yes, and it holds several works" even when no source lists them; a collection is asked its language, category and tags once for all its works; a book page for an anthology opens "Works in this book", one row per work (original title, language, date, category, tags) with "same for all" boxes; found dates show in the contents step marked "from Wikidata", accepted together with "Use these dates"; search shows short pieces only in one folded line under the results ("Also inside 2 books: …"), so everyday searches stay plain; and a known set shows which volumes are missing. Everyone gets the same adding steps; Jakob chose not to have a shorter way for some devices, except that a step is left out when a laptop source will surely fill it.
- **Collections made easy (2026-11-11, phase 15 step 5, version 1.5.0).** Choices made while building it:
  - "Is this the book?" offers "Yes, and it holds several works" whenever no source listed contents; it leads to the same contents step, titled "Which works does it hold?" with an empty box. A scanned volume of a set gets the same choice. Going back and tapping "Yes, this is it" undoes it.
  - A collection is asked the original language, category and tags once; they are given to each new work it creates (`recordsFromForm`), never to a work already in the library, and never an original title, which belongs to one work. The original title box is left out of the language step for a collection.
  - "Works in this book" is reached from a link under a collection's contents on the book page ("Edit the works") and from the entry form, and opens at `#/edit/<edition id>/works`, loaded by the entry form's screen, so the start-up code grows only by the link (219 bytes remain). "Same for all" fills only a work whose own box is empty, so one work in another language can differ. A work with several datings shows them but leaves them as they are; they are changed on their own in an entry form. Changing which works a book holds stays in the entry form's contents box.
  - Saving a book's contents or a single work now sends to the trash any work the book no longer lists that no other book holds (`usedElsewhere`); a work another book holds is kept. Before, turning a book into a collection left its first work behind.
- **The original-text marker (2026-11-12, phase 15 step 7, on the `known-sets` branch).** Beside a single work's other editions, the book page names which of our books print it in its original language ("Ancient Greek text: Plato I"), from each edition's languages ("Greek" counts for "Ancient Greek"); nothing is stored (`originalTextHolders` in `core/library.js`). It shows only when the work has other editions, where comparing them matters. To make room at start-up, the primary button style moved to `styles/screens.css` (only screens loaded when opened use it), a piece on the book page shows its title and date but not its author (the book's own), and the folded search line names its first piece and how many more. 48 bytes remain.
- **Known sets while adding, and set gaps (2026-11-12, phase 15 step 8).**
  - A scanned volume found in a known set by its own ISBN takes its title, authors and works from the list at once. An ISBN the whole set shares (Great Books) first asks "Which volume of … is this?", before "Is this the book?", so the book shown is the right volume; what the lookup said about another volume (its title, authors, contents, pages, subjects and contributors) is set aside. "Not in the list" goes on with the details typed by hand. While adding a set whose title matches a known set, the volume step offers the list's volumes.
  - The contents step for a listed volume says the works come from the list; a line left as the list gives it keeps the list's original title, language, translator and pages, and a line a person changed is saved as typed. Wikidata is not asked about a listed volume's works, since the list gives their dates. When all its works share one language or one translator, those are filled in for the language and translation steps.
  - Set gaps are on the Wishlist, which loads only when opened, because the start-up code has 48 bytes left. Each known set we hold a volume of shows a folded line ("Great Books of the Western World: 12 of 60 held · missing 1, 3, 5 …"), and "Put the missing on the wishlist" adds one item per missing volume, titled with the set, volume and its title, skipping volumes already wished for.
  - A scan of an ISBN a whole known set shares skips "You already have this edition": every volume of Great Books prints the same ISBN, so that question would have stopped the second volume. Instead, "Which volume?" marks each volume already in the library ("Already in the library: this adds another copy"). Found while trying the release in the preview.
- **Value by any level of a location (2026-11-12, Jakob's request).** Settings › Value has a row of buttons, "Totals by: Place, Room, Bookcase, Shelf" (Room as before unless another is chosen, kept while the app is open), and the insurance CSV totals by the same level. A book with a shorter location is totalled at its own deepest level ("Home › Bedroom" among the bookcases). The Library was left as it was: its Location sort already orders place, room, bookcase and shelf in turn, so a sort for each level would give the same order, and the Location filter already picks any level. Grouped headings in the Library, or keeping shelf order within a shelf, would need start-up code the phone budget has no room for (48 bytes free); the shelf view shows a shelf in its real order.
