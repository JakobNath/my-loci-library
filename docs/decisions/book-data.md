# Decisions: Book data: lookups, search and the three sources

Moved word for word from `HOW_IT_WORKS.md` on 10 November 2026, in their original order (oldest first). Add new decisions on this topic at the end, in the same form: a bold title with the date, then the choice and the reason.

- **Reading lookup answers (2026-11-09).** Pages are taken as printed ("xxviii, 311") and library placeholders ("pages cm", printed before a book's page count was known) are ignored in favour of the plain count. Translators, editors, introductions and illustrators are read from Open Library's "by" line; names keep their initials ("F.J. Sheed").
- **Catalog heights check the record's ISBN (2026-11-10).** The Library of Congress service sometimes answers a search with records for other books, so a height is taken only from a record that lists the book's own ISBN (as ISBN-10 or ISBN-13).
- **Book search shows editions, ranked by the words typed (2026-11-10, Jakob's notes).** Searching by title for the Wishlist or Add found Hackett's *Complete Works of Plato* without its author, and could not find *Anna Karenina* in Everyman's Library. Open Library names many works in their original language ("Анна Каренина") and some authors in Greek ("Πλάτων"), and a publisher or series typed into the search ("everyman's library") is in no title, so the right book fell out of the results. `searchOpenLibrary` now asks for each work's best-matching edition (English titles preferred), leaves out little words (of, the, and), and also asks with the last word or two as the publisher (`searchQueries` in `core/lookup.js`); the answers are merged and ranked by how many of the typed words each edition's title, authors and publisher hold. Each result shows its publisher and year, and picking one fills that exact edition by its ISBN. Three requests are made at once; together they take about half a second.
- **Better book data planned (2026-11-10).** After testing the Library of Congress, Open Library and Google Books against six books of the sample shelf, Jakob chose to use all three: richer entries at scan time on the phone (Open Library's full edition record, and Google Books with a key on each device), a nightly `loci-enrich` on the laptop (replacing `loci-sizes`) that fills empty fields from the Library of Congress first and reports, anthology contents offered for confirmation but never for Bibles, an easy Google Books key, and data format version 2 for catalogue numbers and subjects, last. The design is in PLAN.md ("Book data") and the steps are phase 14 of the build plan; the shelf view review (13.0) waits for real books and does not block it.
- **Recorded answers for book data (2026-11-10, build step 14.1).** `core/tests/answers/` holds what Open Library (the full edition record, its work record, and the answer used when adding a book), the Library of Congress and Google Books (its search and the volume's own record) said about nine ISBNs, each as the text the source sent, so the readers in `core` can be tested in the browser and in Node without a connection. The build plan's eight books were joined by Holmes's *Apostolic Fathers in English*, because none of the eight has a contents note in the Library of Congress. A Google Books search by ISBN also returns library scans without an ISBN, and sometimes an unrelated book carrying the same ISBN (*Democracy in America* for the Everyman *Anna Karenina*), so the volume recorded, and later read, is the first one that lists the book's own ISBN. Three of the nine are not in the Library of Congress (the Loeb Plato, the Everyman Tolstoy and NA28).
- **Reading the three sources (2026-11-10, build step 14.2).** `core/marc.js` reads a Library of Congress record and `core/book-data.js` reads Open Library's edition record and Google Books' volume record; all three give the same shape as the lookups already made when adding a book (`Found` in `core/lookup.js`), and `mergeFound` takes each field from the first source that has it, in the order Library of Congress, Open Library, Google Books, except that pages as printed beat a bare page count from any source. Choices made while reading the recorded answers:
  - Catalogue punctuation and brackets are removed, and catalogue titles, which are in sentence case, are put into title case ("The letters of Ignatius" becomes "The Letters of Ignatius"); original titles are kept as catalogued ("Prestuplenie i nakazanie").
  - The place keeps its state ("Indianapolis, Ind.", "St. Louis, MO"), as the catalogue gives it and as Chicago allows; dropping it would make Cambridge, Massachusetts, and Cambridge in England the same. "Pub." in a catalogue's publisher is written out ("Hackett Publishing").
  - In a "by" line, the roles named before "by" go to every name after it: "translated and edited by Michael W. Holmes" makes him translator and editor. Someone who "translated, with an introduction and notes" is only the translator, since the introduction is part of that work; an associate editor is recorded with the role "other", so citations name only the book's editor.
  - A uniform title that names a kind of collection ("Works", "Selections") is not an original title, and neither is one that only repeats the title.
  - Subject headings are taken from the Library of Congress's own headings for people, bodies, titles, topics and places (fields 600, 610, 611, 630, 650 and 651), not only topics (650), so that Augustine's *Confessions* is filed under Augustine too.
  - Google Books sometimes gives sizes in the wrong order (Hackett's Plato: 24 cm high, 9.5 cm wide, 16.1 cm thick), so a thickness is kept only when it is less than the width and less than half the height.
  - Open Library's table of contents is read at its top level only, without front and back matter (preface, introduction, notes, index). For *Mere Christianity* this gives its four books; whether a book is an anthology is left to the person, who is asked before anything is created (step 14.4).
  - A set described by its volumes ("v. <1-3>") has no pages as printed; this also applies to the lookups already made when adding a book.
- **Fuller entries when adding (2026-11-10, build step 14.4).** Adding a book now asks Open Library for the full edition record, and Google Books for the volume record whenever this device has a key, at the same moment. Choices made while building it:
  - Open Library's edition record is asked for in the same request as before, with `jscmd=details` instead of `jscmd=data`. Asking for the book and its edition record (`/isbn/<isbn>.json`) as two requests took twice as long, because Open Library answers one request from a device at a time and the edition address redirects once. The edition record sometimes names no authors (Hackett's Plato); they are then taken from Open Library's work search, which adding a book already made for its date suggestions, so the number of requests is the same as before (measured: a median of 650 ms before, 668 ms now).
  - With a key, Google Books is asked alongside Open Library, and the volume's own record after its search; without a key it is asked, as before, only when Open Library lacks an author or publisher, since Google refuses most keyless requests.
  - Heights and thicknesses from Open Library or Google Books are stored with the source `catalog`, the only stored source that is not a person or a photo; a height typed on the spine photo is `measured` and wins.
  - The binding the record names is chosen in advance on the binding step, which still shows, so it can be changed with one tap.
  - When Open Library's cover is missing or too small and Google Books has one, the Google Books cover is kept, waiting for the laptop, as decided in phase 4.
  - When a record lists contents and the book is not a Bible (`core/bible.js`), a step asks "Is this a collection of works?" with the list filled in, one work per line, to correct; works are created only when the person answers yes. A collection skips "Same work?" and the original date, since each of its works has its own date, found later through "Needs an original date".
- **Testing a Google Books key (2026-11-10, build step 14.5).** "Test the key" in Settings › Advanced makes one lookup (Chadwick's *Confessions*) with the key in the box and reads Google's reason for any refusal (`googleKeyVerdict` in `core/book-data.js`): an unknown key, the Books API not switched on, a key limited to other services or to certain websites or apps, or the day's limit reached, each with what to change. The steps for making a key are beside it, in the tools README and in `docs/plan/FOR_JAKOB.md`; they tell people to leave "Application restrictions" at "None", because a phone app at a web address and a laptop tool cannot share a key limited to one website.
- **loci-enrich (2026-11-10, build step 14.6).** It replaces `loci-sizes` and runs with every daily backup. Choices made while building it:
  - Each book is asked once, and again after 90 days, not only when no source knew it: at about 2,000 physical books that is some 22 books a day, and it finds catalog records made since. What it wrote is kept per book in `integrations/enrich.json`; a field it once wrote is never written again, so a value a person changed or cleared stays as they left it. The per-book file also lets a first long run, or one limited with `--limit`, carry on where it stopped.
  - Translators and editors are filled only when the book names no contributors at all, so a list a person began is never added to. The work's original title and language are filled only for a book holding one work.
  - Without a key in `config.json`, Google Books is not asked: it refuses most keyless requests, and the report says so.
  - The three services are asked at the same moment for each book (they are different services), with a second's pause between books. The report is printed and kept in `loci-enrich/last-report.txt` on the laptop.
  - The first trial's report showed four things now handled in `core`: sizes that cannot be one book's are not used at all, since Open Library's record for an Ante-Nicene Fathers volume measured the boxed set (14.1 × 10 × 8.5 inches); volume and part labels ("V. 1. Part I") and front matter are never offered as works; a bookseller's "New Ed edition" reads "New ed."; and a series that only repeats the book's set (Chemnitz's Works) is not filled.
- **Anthologies and lasting sources planned (2026-11-11, Jakob).** While adding the Everyman Montaigne, Jakob asked that adding be easy enough for anyone, that entries be as full and exact as possible at once, and that every work inside a book be findable. The Library of Congress, HathiTrust, Open Library and Wikidata were tested live against his own sets (`handoffs/2026-11-12-sources-and-anthologies-phases.md` in the private workspace holds the answers). Catalogs list the contents of Loeb volumes and Great Books, but not of Library of America, Everyman's Library, Folio or Luther's Works volumes; no source gives the date a work was written except Wikidata, which was right for every famous work tested. Chosen, and written into PLAN.md: HathiTrust and Wikidata become optional phone sources beside Open Library and Google Books, because both allow calls from a web page and a book's first entry should already be full; the Library of Congress stays on the laptop (no encrypted connection, no calls from web pages), nightly and by its one-click shortcut. Works, not chapters; short pieces (poems, hymns, sermons, letters) are kept light inside their book; checked "known set" contents lists for large sets; Wikidata dates confirmed with one tap; a contents-page photo for books no source knows; and works linked across editions only by Wikidata ID or a checked list. The work is phase 15 in PLAN.md, in steps listed in STATUS.md.
- **An outside review of the sources' future (2026-11-11).** Jakob brought a research brief, "10-Year Source Future-Proofing Brief" (kept as `inputs/source-future-proofing-brief.md` in the private workspace), written without knowledge of this project. Adopted from it: standard SPARQL only for Wikidata, since its query service is moving to a backend without the label service (old address until at least February 2027); the endpoint addresses and the app's identity kept in one place; identifying the app to Wikimedia (`Api-User-Agent` from the phone, a User-Agent with a contact from the laptop) and backing off when refused; the laptop keeping each source's raw answer and which source filled which field in the private data repository; lookups by LCCN or OCLC number for books without an ISBN; never joining two records on a guess; HathiTrust links to free full-view scans; and `SOURCES.md`, checked yearly. Not adopted, with the reasons: lookups on the laptop only (adding would give bare entries, and the sources the phone uses allow it); provenance inside every field of every record (a rewrite of every record, the app and the tools, for what a file on the laptop gives); a free list of outside identifiers (fixed, named keys read the same in every file in 2045); DuckDB or SQLite for bulk matching (the tools use only Node's own modules; offline bulk matching is deferred); a Logos `library.xml` (the real Logos export is a CSV, and works); AI matching (never in the app); and language codes in place of names (deferred).
- **Google Books' branding rules, an open question (2026-11-11).** Google's branding page (checked 11 November 2026) asks that its results be attributed and not reordered or mixed with other services' results. Loci names Google Books among the sources a book was found on, but merges its facts with Open Library's into our own record (since 1.1.0). Whether that fits the rules for a private catalog is left to Jakob; nothing was changed. The nine raw Google answers kept for tests stay in the public app, by Jakob's choice, and no new ones are added to a public repository.
- **HathiTrust while adding (2026-11-11, phase 15 step 3, version 1.4.0).** The phone asks HathiTrust's Bibliographic API at the same moment as Open Library (and Google Books with a key), so adding is no slower; `loci-enrich` asks it too, after the Library of Congress. Choices made while building it:
  - Its answer holds MARC records, read by the same code as the Library of Congress (`fromMarc` in `core/marc.js`). Several records may list one ISBN: a record for that volume alone is read before a record for a whole set.
  - A set record ("12 v.", the ISBN noted "(v. 1)") gives the set's title as `set`, the volume as `volume`, and only that volume's works, cut from its contents note by the volume labels ("1.", "[v. 1]", "I.", "V-VI."); its title and its count of volumes are not given as the book's title and pages. When no other source knew the book, it is called "<set>, vol. <n>".
  - A volume record whose title is a list of short titles ("Euthyphro, Apology, Crito, Phaedo, Phaedrus") offers them as its works, each by the book's author when it has one; "Books 1-12" is part of one work, not a work.
  - When records of different books list the same ISBN (Great Books of the Western World, 1990, prints one ISBN on every volume), HathiTrust's answer is not used at all, rather than one volume's at random. Telling which volume was scanned waits for the known sets (step 8).
  - On the phone, Open Library's facts come first and HathiTrust fills only what it lacks, because a library record found by ISBN may describe an older printing (a Loeb volume reprinted since 1914 keeps its ISBN); but the works inside are taken from a library record that lists them (`withLibraryContents`), since contents notes name works where Open Library's tables of contents often name chapters. On the laptop the order stays Library of Congress, HathiTrust, Open Library, Google Books, filling empty fields only.
  - "2 v." is no longer read as pages from any source.
  - The free full-view scan link waits for format version 3 (step 6), which gives an edition its HathiTrust ID; without a stored ID the app would have to ask HathiTrust each time the book is opened.
  - A Settings switch, "And in HathiTrust", beside the other two; a catalog's settings saved before it count it as on.
