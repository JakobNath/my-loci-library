  • Covers vary a little (0.63 to 0.75), spines vary a lot (0.05 to 0.24, five times). Any layout that forces one shape will either crop titles or leave odd gaps.
  • Three kinds of hard photo: light book on a light surface (The Prince or The Two Towers on the white duvet), dark book on a dark surface (the UBS or CTQ spine on a dark table), and glare on glossy jackets.
  • Thin spines are tiny. The Prince's spine is 93 × 2,000 pixels; at list size it is a 14-pixel strip. It needs a minimum width on screen and a tall photo to stay readable.
  • Already-cropped photos must just work. Most of yours are cropped by the phone's editor, so the app should accept a cropped photo as it is.
  • Your phone's editor finds the book for you and lets you fix the corners with a magnifier. That is the bar to meet, inside the app, without leaving it.

2Finding the book automatically

Without AI or any library, the app can do what your phone's editor does in most cases. It learns the background color from the edges of the photo, marks everything that differs from it, keeps the largest solid shape near the middle, fits four corners to it, and straightens it. It is about 250 lines of plain JavaScript, loaded only on the camera screen.

Below, it runs live in your browser on your real Concordia Theological Quarterly photo and on test photos made from your other books (each placed on your duvet, or on a dark mat, at a slight angle). A green result means it would save without asking; honey means it would show the corners for a quick check; red means it would ask you to place them.

4The capture flow in the app

The single most important choice for speed: one camera session for a whole stack. The camera opens once and stays open. Barcode, cover and spine follow each other by themselves, and the next book starts straight away. There are no buttons to find between steps, no switching to the phone's own camera, and no editing unless something is off.

5Showing photos of every shape

Seven real books, four ways to lay them out. The rule that matters most for a modern, calm look: never crop a book's photo to fit a box. Let each book keep its shape, line them up on a common baseline like books on a shelf, and keep the same soft shadow and book-like corners on all of them.

Recommendation

  1. In-app camera, one session per stack: barcode, cover, spine, next book, with a live outline and automatic capture when the book is found and held still. A shutter button and "Skip" are always there.
  2. Automatic finding and straightening with the plain-JavaScript method above. Clear results save straight away; doubtful ones show the corners for a two-second check, with a magnifier while dragging, like your phone's editor.
  3. A two-sided photo mat (a sheet of poster board, white on one side and dark on the other) where you scan. When the app sees too little contrast it says "Try the other side of the mat". This one habit turns almost every hard case into an easy one.
  4. Import from Photos for pictures already taken, as you do now: select several, and the app crops each one and pairs them with the books in the stack.
  5. Photos keep their real shape everywhere. The Covers grid uses "standing books" (fixed column width, natural height, aligned at the bottom). On a laptop, justified rows. Spines show at their true thickness with a small minimum width so thin ones stay tappable.
  6. Sizes: covers saved 600 px tall (plus a 200 px thumbnail), spines 900 px tall (plus 300 px), as JPEG, as already planned. A tiny average color is saved with each photo for the placeholder shown while it loads.
