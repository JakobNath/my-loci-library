## Phone test results

Results from `prototypes/phone-test.html`, run on real phones during the design stop.

- **Android 16, Firefox 159 (2026-11-09).** Firefox has no built-in barcode detector, so the bundled decoder was used. It loaded in 345 ms the first time and 65 ms once cached. It read ISBN 9780802867339 correctly in 9.5 s on the first try, including the time spent aiming, and in 2.0 s on the second try. Storage passed: persistent storage was granted, the quota was about 56 GB, 50 MB was written in 1.1 s and read back intact in 0.3 s.
- **iPhone, iOS 18.7, Safari (2026-11-09).** Safari has no built-in barcode detector switched on, so the bundled decoder was used. It loaded in 18 ms and read ISBN 9780802867339 correctly in 1.1 s. Storage passed: the quota was about 39 GB, 50 MB was written in 1.0 s and read back intact in 0.04 s. Persistent storage was not granted in an ordinary Safari tab, which is normal; the plan relies on the home-screen app, which Safari exempts from its seven-day clearing of site data. No change of decoder is needed.

Note for phase 6: the first scan took a while. Things to try then are asking the camera for continuous focus where supported and decoding only the middle band of the frame, where the barcode sits.

## Decisions

Choices made during the build that the plan did not settle, with the reason for each.

- **No GitHub CLI at the start (2026-11-08).** `gh` was not installed when the build began. All local work for phases 1 and 2 went ahead, and the GitHub repositories were created once `gh` was installed and signed in.
- **Line endings.** Every repository has a `.gitattributes` file that stores text with Unix line endings (LF), so files read the same on Windows and Linux.
- **Local preview server.** ES modules do not load from `file://` addresses, so during development the app is served with any static server (for example `python -m http.server`). Nothing in the app depends on which server is used.
- **Barcode decoder: zxing-wasm 3.1.6, reader build (2026-11-08).** The smallest reliable choice that reads EAN-13 in Safari on iPhone, where the browser's own `BarcodeDetector` is still switched off. It is about 1 MB in total and loads only on the scan screen. Android Chrome uses the browser's own detector when it supports EAN-13. Details and the one setting that must never be removed (pointing it at the local `.wasm` file) are in `vendor/README.md`.
- **Prototypes share one engine.** The five design options in `prototypes/` all use `prototypes/shared/proto.js` for behaviour and `prototypes/shared/base.css` for structure. Each option differs only in its own `tokens.css` and `option.css`, so the comparison is about the look, not about different features. The prototypes are throwaway and are not reused in the app.
- **Prototype covers are drawn, not photographed.** Each sample book is shown as a plain tile in roughly the color of its binding, so no outside images are fetched.
- **GitHub Pages serves the files as they are.** An empty `.nojekyll` file in the app repo turns off GitHub's Jekyll processing, so there is no build step on GitHub's side either. Pages publishes the `main` branch from the repository root.
- **Second round of designs (2026-11-09).** Feedback on the first five: options 1 (card catalog) and 3 (warm bookshop) were the favorites, but the look should be more timeless, modern and sleek, with fewer bright digital colors. Options 6 to 8 answer that: refined versions of 1 and 3 and a blend of the two, all in warm neutrals with one muted accent.
- **Third round: a playground (2026-11-09).** Option 7 was the favorite. Rather than guess at more fixed variations, option 9 is option 7 with an Adjust panel that switches colors, paper, title type, shelves, covers, corners, spacing, top bar, laptop menu and logo. Each choice is an attribute on the page that only swaps design tokens, which is the same mechanism the finished app will use, so whatever is chosen can be copied straight into `styles/tokens.css`. The current look is kept in the page address so it can be shared as a link. Ten new "L" logo ideas (modern, cute, classic) were in `prototypes/shared/l-logos.js` (removed when the design was locked; see the Git history).
- **Favorite look (2026-11-09).** Chosen in the playground: forest green main color, honey pop, ivory paper, book serif titles, no shelves, large covers, soft cover shadows, round corners, cozy spacing, colored top bar, menu at the side, bookplate logo. Saved as the "Favorite" starting look in `prototypes/option-9/panel.js` (since removed). Nine more classic L logos were drawn to choose from; the logo is still open.
- **Design locked (2026-11-09).** The favorite look with the ex libris logo was chosen. Its values are now in `styles/tokens.css` and described in `docs/plan/DESIGN.md`. In dark mode the main green was made a deeper forest green as asked, with a separate lighter green for green text so contrast stays above 4.5:1. All other prototype options, the Adjust panel and the unused logo ideas were removed to keep the repository small; they remain in the Git history. One prototype is left, in `prototypes/design/`, and it reads `styles/tokens.css` directly, so it always shows the current design.
- **Photo ideas page (2026-11-09).** At Jakob's request, `prototypes/photos/` explores spine photos alongside covers, with mockups for capture, Library views, the book page, lists, sets and fallbacks. It uses one real cover and spine (Luther's Works volume 4), shrunk in the browser to the planned sizes: cover 3 KB + 48 KB, spine 4 KB + 33 KB. Nothing from it is in the plan yet; `PLAN.md` changes only once the ideas are chosen.
- **Photo capture prototype (2026-11-09).** `prototypes/photos/capture.html` tests finding and straightening a book in a photo with plain JavaScript (`prototypes/shared/autocrop.js`, no AI, no library): learn the background from the photo's edges, try a ladder of thresholds, keep the cleanest central shape, check that its four edges are real edges, then straighten with a perspective transform. On the test set, 9 of 11 photos were found without help; the two light-on-light cases correctly ask for a corner check, and none were saved wrongly. A two-sided photo mat (light and dark) fixes the hard cases. Not in the plan until chosen.
- **Photo lab, round 3 (2026-11-09).** `prototypes/photos/lab.html` replaces composite test photos with real ones only (`prototypes/photos/real/photos.json`, filled from `inputs/photos/`), and measures success against true edges that a person confirms in the page's review step (`real/truth.json`). New: a spine mode that finds two long parallel edges (`shared/spine.js`); edge refinement at full resolution with an error estimate in pixels (`shared/refine.js`), which decides saving without asking (within 2% of a cover's width, 3% of a spine's); an edge-line editor for spines with snapping and a rotate handle (`photos/editor.js`); and enhancement using the surface around the book as a neutral reference (`shared/enhance.js`). Laptop timings in the development browser pane were throttled about sevenfold, so speed is reported from phones. Nothing here is in `PLAN.md` or `DATA_FORMAT.md` until approved.
- **Real test photos (2026-11-09).** 40 photos from `inputs/photos/` are in `prototypes/photos/real/` (reduced to 1,600 px; about 10 MB with the finished images made from them). 29 are uncropped (14 covers, 15 spines; wood table in daylight, one on the duvet) and 11 were already cropped in a phone editor. Checked by eye before review: 12 of 14 covers and 1 of 15 spines saved without asking, none wrongly. Fixes from the real photos: a spine is no longer saved when something (usually a hand) lies across one of its ends; a photo without a camera's exact shape is taken as already cropped; only a light surface is trusted as white for white balance, because dark wood and a dark grey mat cannot be told apart; the even-lighting correction is gentler. The 17 photographed books now appear, with their real photos, in the design prototype and the photo views. Before a release, the test photos could move out of the app repository to keep it small.
- **Measured against the reviewed outlines (2026-11-09).** Jakob reviewed the true edges of all 40 photos (`prototypes/photos/real/truth.json`). Covers: 11 of 14 right without asking (79%), 1 wrong. Spines: 0 of 15 right without asking, 1 wrong, 8 asked that were right. Both wrong saves are the same library book in a clear plastic jacket: the outline followed the plastic, 7.6% of the width outside the board, while the estimated error was 0.2%. A check for a second edge just inside the first was tried and not kept, because a hardcover's hinge groove gives the same signal and 5 of 11 good covers would have asked. Tightening the error bar would not help either, since the edge found is sharp; it is just the wrong one. The estimate is therefore a guard against blurred or broken edges, not against a wrong edge; the result thumbnail with one-tap re-crop remains the safeguard. Spine rates need photos taken lying flat with nothing touching the ends, which this set does not have.
